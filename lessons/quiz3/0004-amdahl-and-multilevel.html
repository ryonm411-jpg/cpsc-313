Amdahl's Law & Multi-level Caches

A speedup is limited by the part you did not improve.

Your win for today: apply Amdahl's Law forwards and backwards, and add up multi-level miss times (R3.9). About 20 minutes.

Warm-up: recall

Hit 3 ns, total miss 53 ns, 90% hit rate. Average access time in ns?

0.9 × 3 + 0.1 × 53 = 2.7 + 5.3 = 8. Lesson 1.

1 · The law

On your reference sheet Tnew = Told × ((1 − a) + a/k)
speedup = 1 / ((1 − a) + a/k)
a = fraction of the old time in the improved part; k = how many times faster that part gets.

Slide example: 100 s, 25% in memory instructions, memory twice as fast. Speedup = 1/(0.75 + 0.125) = 1.14, and Tnew = 87.5 s. Slides 3.4

Make k infinite and a/k vanishes, so the limit is 1/(1 − a). That is why 256 threads barely beat 4 when most of the work is sequential.

60% of runtime gets 3× faster. Speedup? (2 decimals)

1 / (0.4 + 0.6/3) = 1 / 0.6 = 1.67.

Same program, but the 60% part gets infinitely fast. Speedup limit? (2 decimals)

1 / 0.4 = 2.5.

40% of runtime can be improved. What k gives an overall speedup of 1.25?

1/1.25 = 0.8 = 0.6 + 0.4/k, so 0.4/k = 0.2 and k = 2.

20% of runtime can be improved. Can you get an overall speedup of 1.5?

  1. Yes, with a large enough k
  2. Yes, if k is exactly 5.00
  3. No, the limit is 1.25 here
  4. No, the limit is 1.20 here

The limit is 1/0.8 = 1.25. A question like this may ask you to enter -1 when no answer exists, as R3.8 does.

2 · Multi-level caches: follow the line

Write-back L1 and L2, with data flowing memory ↔ L2 ↔ L1. Use L1 hit 3, L1↔L2 transfer 20, L2↔memory transfer 50. Ask: which transfers must happen? Add them, then add the final L1 access.

Read caseStepsTotal
L1 hit33
L1 miss (clean), L2 hit20 + 323
L1 miss (dirty), L2 hit20 + 20 + 343
L1 miss (clean), L2 miss (clean)50 + 20 + 373
L1 miss (clean), L2 miss (dirty)50 + 50 + 20 + 3123

A dirty eviction adds one extra transfer on that link, to write the old line down. These costs follow the rule in slides 3.5.

70% of accesses hit L1. Of those that miss, 90% hit L2. All evictions are clean. Average access time in ns? (2 decimals)

0.7×3 + 0.3×(0.9×23 + 0.1×73) = 2.1 + 0.3×28 = 10.5. “Of those that miss” is a nested percentage: the 90% applies only inside the 0.3.
