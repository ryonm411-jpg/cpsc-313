Pipeline Hazards Cheat Sheet

y86-64, 5 stages: F · D · E · M · W. Prints on one or two pages.

Timing facts

  • Consumer needs its operands by the end of Decode.
  • valE (ALU result) first exists at the end of Execute. valM (loaded value, from mrmovq or popq rA) first exists at the end of Memory.
  • A register written in W is readable in Decode the next cycle.
  • Condition codes are set in E and read by the next instruction's E. No hazard.

Data hazards: distance rule

Distance = how many instructions after the producer the consumer comes (back-to-back = 1; nops count).

Predictors

  • Never taken: next PC = valP. ≈40% right.
  • Always taken: next PC = valC. ≈60% right. Keeps valP (as M_valA) for recovery.
  • BTFNT: backward taken (loops), forward not taken (ifs). ≈65%.
  • Fancier: 1-bit history, per-PC tables. Delay slots on some ISAs.

CPI

CPI = (I + lost) / I

I = instructions executed incl. halt, excluding squashed ones. lost = stall cycles + squashed instructions. (CS:APP §4.5.9.) Check your course's answer key on whether pipeline fill cycles are included.

Counting procedure

  1. Trace register values and condition codes → actual outcome of each jXX.
  2. Walk the executed path only.
  3. Data: nearest prior writer of each source register → distance table.
  4. Control: jXX 2 (unless predicted right), ret 3.
  5. Sum. Watch for overlap: an earlier stall gives later instructions extra time.

Terms

Stall
Holding an instruction in its stage (F and D keep their values) while a bubble goes into E.
Bubble
A nop injected into a stage so nothing useful happens there that cycle.
Forwarding
Sending a value straight from a later stage back to Decode, skipping the wait for the register file. Also called bypassing.
Load/use hazard
A loaded value used by the very next instruction. It needs 1 stall even with forwarding.
Squash
Cancelling wrong-path instructions after a misprediction by turning them into bubbles. Also called quash or cancel.
Distance
How many instructions separate consumer from producer (back-to-back = 1).
